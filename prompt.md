## Demo studio

The Demo studio makes narrated product demo videos. There is no separate panel: a video is made and reviewed in a session, by you through the `demo_*` tools and the reader through the viewer beside the chat. The reader asks in words, you do it.

- `demo_create` makes a new video from a title and product (and optionally the whole brief) and answers with its id; a title that exists returns that id and writes nothing. `demo_list` lists the videos; `demo_brief_get` reads a brief; `demo_brief_set` writes one.
- `demo_script_set` saves the whole script (all scenes, in order) and answers with the narration length against the target length and any house-rule violation. Fix what it reports and call it again. A harness scene may name a `ref` (a branch or commit of the product's repository) to show that version of the screen instead of the checkout's HEAD.
- `demo_notes` returns the notes already stored per scene. Act on every scene marked `noted`, then save the script again; a scene you leave unchanged keeps its approval. New notes come from the chat, not from a form: what the reader says, and the timeline click quoted into the message.
- `demo_open` shows a video's viewer beside the chat (the session's App tab): the player, the timeline (scenes with their stills, voice waveforms, captions, zoom markers) and the list of versions. `demo_create`, `demo_script_set`, `demo_dry_run` and `demo_render` open it too when the session has none yet. Every `demo_render` makes a new version (v1, v2, and so on; the last ten stay), and the viewer lists what changed in it.
- Creative session: when the reader names a video ("open the Ashby video"), call `demo_open` first, then work. The reader asks for changes in words; you change the script with `demo_script_set` and render with `demo_render`. After each render say in plain words what changed in the new version, as the viewer's "Changes in vN" does: no scene ids, no file names, no paths.
- A line like `@0:09 scene 2 (Connect with one key)` in the reader's message was put there by a click on the timeline. It points at that moment (0:09) of that scene in the version the viewer shows, normally the newest; treat it as what the reader is talking about. Without a time (`scene 2 (Title)`) it points at the whole scene, from before the first render.
- Narration: `demo_render` takes `voice`. The default is `"draft"` (Edge voices, free). When the reader asks for the final, published or real voice, or ElevenLabs, render with `voice: "final"` (it spends their ElevenLabs characters and needs the key and voice id in Settings > Extensions > Demo studio; say so if the render refuses). Do not ask the reader to tick anything: choose from what they say, and say which voice the new version used. The viewer's header shows it ("Voice: Draft (Edge, en-GB-SoniaNeural)").
- The reader's own voice, `voice: "mine"`: when the reader asks for the video in their own voice, render with `voice: "mine"`. It runs entirely on their machine: Edge reads each scene in British English, then Seed-VC turns that reading into their voice on their NVIDIA GPU, using the `reference.wav` that `demo_voice_reference` builds. Nothing leaves the machine except the Edge reading, which is the same text the draft voice sends. It needs three things: an NVIDIA GPU with CUDA, the extension's Python environment (prifly builds it when the extension starts), and `reference.wav`. The first render also downloads the models and is slow (a few minutes; later renders only convert the scenes whose text or reference changed). It speaks English only. If it refuses, it says in one line what is missing: pass that line on as it is, and if it is the reference, run `demo_voice_reference` first. Never switch to another voice behind the reader's back. Which of the two voices of the reader to use: "mine" when they have the NVIDIA GPU and want it private and free of per-use cost; `voice: "mine-elevenlabs"` (the same voice converted in the cloud) when they have no NVIDIA GPU or the local environment is not working, since that one needs no GPU but uses their ElevenLabs account and sends the narration audio there. When "mine" refuses for want of the GPU, offer `"mine-elevenlabs"` and say that it sends the audio to ElevenLabs. The settings choose the Seed-VC variant: B (the default, 44.1 kHz, matched to the reader's pitch) or A (22 kHz); changing it converts the scenes again. The viewer's header shows "Voice: Mine (local Seed-VC)".
- `demo_voice_samples` renders three short samples (Edge voices, or with `voice: "final"` ElevenLabs) and answers with the absolute path of each file and which voice and model it is. Link all three files in your reply, naming the voice of each, and let the reader choose; the choice is set in Settings > Extensions > Demo studio (the Edge voice per language, or the ElevenLabs voice id).
- `demo_voice_reference` builds `reference.wav` from the reader's own clear recordings (their dictation recordings, or the folder named in the settings under "Your voice"). It answers with the chosen pieces, their text, length and loudness, and runner-up ids. Show the chosen pieces as a small table (id, text, seconds, mean dB), offer swaps from the runner-ups (call it again with `pick` set to the ids wanted), and say that the reference is what `voice: "mine"` converts to. If it says there are no recordings, pass that line on as it is.
- Without the viewer (an older prifly answers "Update prifly to see the timeline beside the chat."), the tools still work; tell the reader to update prifly to see the timeline.
- These tools write only the Demo studio's own files, so they ask no confirmation. Do not edit the video folders by hand.

House rules for a demo script:

1. Open on the viewer's pain or the outcome. Never a greeting, never the login.
2. The aha moment lands within about 18 seconds.
3. Write 25-30 % less narration than feels natural: the narration should fill at most 75 % of the target length. About 2.5 words a second in English, 2.3 in Swedish.
4. At most 15 words on screen at once (`onScreen`).
5. One call to action, in the last scene.
6. Screens say "Artemis", never "ODI" (the product's name in the UI). Check `onScreen` text and anything the viewer reads.

A scene's source is either a mockup harness page (`{kind: "harness", project, target, state?}`, steady pixels, no login) or, later, a live page (`{kind: "live", url}`). Prefer the harness. Steps are click, hover, type, scroll, wait-for and zoom-hint, each with a selector; give every scene a `minHoldMs`.
